Hyde

Greetings, my name’s Alexis. On this website you will find a range of my written work. Some are academic assignments, while others I wrote for fun. In the grand scheme of all projects, however, my mission is to advocate for a new philosophy—one that is better suited than traditional theories—on autonomy and protecting the rights of the person in the always-advancing age of artificial intelligence. Constantly do questions about institutional power, individual liberties, legal doctrines, and technological design cross my mind. I hope that by reading my work a similar sense of inspiration intuits within you.

So you’ve found my art… I am a lot better at the art of authorship than design, but obviously dabble in the latter anyways. I think in images, remember in abstractions, and deal with a lot of ideas. Sometimes, I prefer drawing out personal events rather than writing them. Other times, as Aristotle once said, “the aim of art is to represent not the outward appearance of things, but their inward significance.” That is essentially what I attempt to do. My process is simple; I use watercolor pencils, so first draw with the pencil and then go back with a watercolor brush. I believe art, like writing, is a constant work in progress. So, many of these are still in the draft phase. In any case, I am no pro but in the words of Pablo Picasso, “art washes away from the soul the dust of everyday life.”

I am a writer primarily in political theory who focuses on assessing policy from various vantage points. My academic background is in political science, where I concentrated my studies in theory, as well as French and global film & media studies. Writing on these subjects really blossomed into a passion of mine particularly from the three years I served as an Editor for my undergraduate political review journal. It was there that I explored creative conventions for addressing dense topics, realizing the great ways in which an author can tailor a unique storytelling experience for their audiences.

To my projects I also offer a strong background in legal and policy work, ranging from land-use laws research to risks posed by AI to the markets and national security interests. That is, I oscillate from writing on what’s actually happening in the real-world to predicting what might happen in the foreseeable future. At times, my writing may seem pessimistic about the fate of the world amid the trajectory of technology. My range of attitudes have certainly evolved much like a pinball shooting back and forth. I believe the best is possible, but take note of (ethical) negligence as I see it. Much of the finger I point at American firms derives inspiration from my French studies, where I learned about progressive institutional efforts to protect privacy such as with the GDPR. What I believe you will find makes me stand out as a writer is the personal voice I add to academic arguments, adding a layer to them as an active witness participating in the unraveling. Thank you for visiting my website!
